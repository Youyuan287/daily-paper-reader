## ABSTRACT 

Query-conditioned vision–language models enable fine-grained interpretation by revealing how visual evidence changes with textual queries. However, evidence conditioned on complete descriptions does not necessarily resolve into object-specific evidence, nor does an exposed evidence map necessarily identify the ev-idence that constitutes the model’s prediction. Across multiple VLM architec-tures and independent benchmarks, we find that object-level queries often retain evidence from co-occurring objects and shared context. In this paper, we intro-duce ProtoLIP , a lightweight prototype-mediated evidence layer that organizes reusable visual prototypes into text-derived semantic families and uses query-dependent family routing to constrain which prototypes may provide evidence. ProtoLIP requires no spatial annotations and does not retrain the backbone. It im-proves evidence localization and separation across query granularities, and its lo-calization gains also transfer to independently pretrained VLMs when their patch and text representations are sufficiently aligned. It remains competitive with a spa-tially supervised grounding model while improving image–text matching. Cru-cially, ProtoLIP constructs its matching score directly from localized prototype evidence, enabling the score to be exactly decomposed into semantic-family and prototype contributions. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Vision–language models (VLMs) learn transferable visual representations through image–text align-ment (Radford et al., 2021; Zhai et al., 2023). Recent work has moved beyond global alignment to-ward finer-grained and text-conditioned visual representations (Zheng et al., 2024; Xiao et al., 2025; Lyu et al., 2026), enabling the same image to be interpreted at different semantic granularities. As queries become more specific, their visual evidence should ideally become correspondingly precise, from complete descriptions to individual objects. However, evidence conditioned on complete descriptions does not necessarily become object-specific when the query is narrowed. Existing objectives may separate rich textual descriptions without requiring their constituent objects or corresponding visual evidence to be disentangled. Consequently, object-level queries can remain tied to co-occurring objects or shared context. In Figure 1, both models produce reasonable evidence for the full caption “a woman riding a bicycle,” yet ItemizedCLIP (Lyu et al., 2026) retains evidence from the woman and background even when queried specifically about the bicycle. Thus, description-level conditioning does not necessarily extend to object-level evidence separation. 1arXiv:2609.16284v1 [cs.CV] 14 Sep 2026 Figure 1: Object-level evidence disentanglement. DreamLIP (Zheng et al., 2024) exploits long captions and sub-caption supervision, FLAIR (Xiao et al., 2025) constructs language-informed visual representations through text-conditioned aggrega-tion, and ItemizedCLIP (Lyu et al., 2026) introduces objectives for item independence and represen-tation completeness. These advances improve correspondence between rich descriptions and visual content, but the unit of textual supervision is not necessarily an individual object. In particular, an ItemizedCLIP item may contain multiple objects and relations, so separation between items need not extend to the constituent objects within an item. Related studies report complementary symptoms of this limitation. SWIM (Sun et al., 2026) finds that object nouns can produce diffuse visual activations, while analyses of CLIP models reveal re-liance on background regions and spurious contextual correlations (Agarwal et al., 2026). These observations motivate our study of evidence disentanglement: whether description-level condition-ing yields spatially separated, object-specific evidence. We use evidence disentanglement to denote the structured separation of predictive evidence across semantic and spatial dimensions. We examine two complementary aspects: separating evidence for a sentence into contributions associated with its constituent objects, and spatially separating object-specific evidence from co-occurring entities and context. For this separation to explain a prediction, the evidence must also be explicitly connected to the matching score. Localization alone does not establish this connection: attention weights need not reflect predictive influence (Wu et al., 2024b;a), and gradient-based attribution methods also face faithfulness and completeness limitations (Mehri et al., 2025). To address these requirements, we introduce ProtoLIP, a lightweight prototype-mediated evidence layer on top of a frozen VLM. Its central design separates coarse semantic routing from fine-grained query scoring: text-derived semantic families constrain which prototypes may contribute, while the complete query evaluates the compatibility of their localized visual evidence. This organization sup-ports object-, phrase-, and sentence-level queries without restricting queries to fixed class labels. The resulting prototype contributions directly and additively constitute the image–text matching score, enabling exact decomposition through semantic families and prototypes without spatial supervision or backbone retraining. 2Our experiments show improved object-specific localization across datasets and query granulari-ties, alongside preserved or improved full-caption localization. On the frozen ItemizedCLIP back-bone, ProtoLIP adds only 1.48% trainable parameters and improves Pointing and Energy by up to 42.2% and 55.1%, respectively. Localization gains also transfer to independently pretrained VLM backbones. Despite using only text-derived weak supervision and no spatial annotations, ProtoLIP remains competitive with a dedicated spatially supervised grounding model. Direct inference im-proves matching separability, with reductions in strict top-1 retrieval; score-level fusion can partially recover retrieval performance while retaining the same spatial evidence maps. Our contributions are: • ProtoLIP, a prototype-mediated evidence layer on top of a frozen vision–language back-bone. Text-derived semantic families provide coarse routing over query-relevant visual pro-totypes, while the complete query performs fine-grained scoring over the routed evidence. The resulting prototype contributions directly and additively constitute the matching score, yielding an exactly decomposable prediction path from localized prototype evidence to the final score. • Improved evidence disentanglement across query granularities, datasets, and VLM back-bones, with stronger localization from object-level queries to complete sentence descrip-tions. Despite using only text-derived weak supervision and no spatial annotations, Pro-toLIP remains competitive with spatially supervised grounding while improving image– text matching. 

## 2 RELATED WORK 

Fine-grained vision–language representations. Contrastive vision–language models such as CLIP (Radford et al., 2021) learn transferable representations through global image–text alignment, while SigLIP (Zhai et al., 2023) improves vision–language pretraining with pairwise sigmoid su-pervision. Subsequent work has pursued finer correspondence between visual and textual content. FILIP (Yao et al., 2022) introduces token-wise interactions between visual and textual representa-tions, while RegionCLIP (Zhong et al., 2022) extends language–image alignment to localized image regions. Other approaches enrich the textual supervision itself: DreamLIP (Zheng et al., 2024) lever-ages long captions and sub-captions to learn more detailed visual–language correspondences. More recent work makes the visual representation explicitly dependent on the textual description. FLAIR (Xiao et al., 2025) uses text-conditioned attention pooling over local visual tokens to produce language-informed representations for fine-grained retrieval and localization. Most closely related to our setting, ItemizedCLIP (Lyu et al., 2026) introduces item-independence and representation-completeness objectives to learn distinct visual representations for multiple semantically indepen-dent textual items describing the same image. Together, these approaches progressively refine the granularity of vision–language alignment and textual conditioning. However, the unit of textual supervision is not necessarily an individual visual object. Long captions, sub-captions, and textual items may each contain multiple objects, attributes, and relations; separating such descriptions therefore does not require their constituent objects to have correspondingly separated visual evidence. ProtoLIP addresses this remaining granularity gap by resolving rich textual descriptions into localized evidence for their individual objects. 

Vision–language localization and grounding. A parallel line of work seeks to recover object-level spatial evidence from pretrained vision–language models. Training-free methods such as NA-CLIP (Hajimiri et al., 2025) derive dense localization maps directly from pretrained CLIP represen-tations without task-specific grounding training. Dedicated grounding models such as Grounding DINO (Liu et al., 2024) and MM Grounding DINO (Zhao et al., 2024), in contrast, use explicit detection and grounding supervision to learn language-conditioned object localization. Our setting differs primarily in supervision. Dedicated grounding models rely on explicit detection or grounding annotations, whereas ProtoLIP uses no spatial annotations and keeps the pretrained VLM backbone frozen. Despite this substantially weaker spatial supervision, ProtoLIP achieves competitive localization against supervised grounding models, while outperforming training-free CLIP localization methods under our shared evaluation protocol. 3Figure 2: Overview of ProtoLIP. Training text is organized offline into coarse semantic families that constrain prototype eligibility. Given an image–text pair, a frozen vision–language backbone extracts patch features and a complete text representation. Prototype-based visual evidence and prediction decomposition. Prototype-based models ex-plain predictions through similarities to learned representative patterns (Chen et al., 2019). Vision– language extensions include class-specific visual prototypes for weakly supervised segmentation (VPL (Xu et al., 2025)) and language-guided part prototypes for interpretable image classification (PRISM (Yu & Yang, 2026)). PRISM uses category-name-conditioned CLIP attribution maps to guide part-prototype learning and aggregates their activations through a fixed-label classifier. Its goal is to explain category predictions, whereas ours is to separate object-specific evidence and decompose image–text matching scores across object, phrase, and sentence queries. To this end, ProtoLIP combines offline LLM-assisted concept extraction with coarse semantic fam-ilies that constrain prototype eligibility for each query. The complete query scores the localized evidence pooled by eligible prototypes, whose contributions sum exactly to the matching score. Thus, language controls both evidence eligibility and compatibility during inference, rather than serving only as training guidance. This design connects object-specific evidence learning with ex-plicit decomposition of sentence-level matching predictions. 

## 3 METHOD 

Given an image–text pair (I, T ), where I is an image and T is a text query, a frozen VLM encodes 

I into patch features X(I) = {xn}Nn=1 and T into a complete-query embedding zT . ProtoLIP adds a prototype-mediated layer to these frozen representations (Fig. 2) to produce a matching score 

S(I, T ) and query-specific spatial evidence. Text-derived semantic families constrain prototype eligibility, while the full query determines their evidence compatibility. Family-level regularization encourages compact and separated evidence. 3.1 TEXT -D ERIVED SEMANTIC FAMILIES 

We construct our main semantic taxonomy from approximately 50% of Itemized-CC0.3M (Lyu et al., 2026). An offline LLM-assisted procedure extracts localizable objects, groups them into 39 semantic families, and normalizes their lexical aliases. The taxonomy organizes 512 prototypes and is frozen before training. Each prototype belongs to exactly one family, so the families partition the prototype bank into sub-banks of 13–14 prototypes. A small number of ambiguous concepts are associated with more than one family, so a single query may activate multiple families, but the sub-banks themselves remain disjoint. The complete prototype bank used by unmatched queries is therefore exactly the union over all families. During training and inference, deterministic lexical matching selects eligible families, while un-matched queries use the complete prototype bank. No LLM is invoked at runtime, and the complete text representation zT retains attributes, actions, modifiers, and relations. 4We also constructed a smaller, 23-family taxonomy (K23) from an earlier 30K-description sample of the training corpus. The two taxonomies yield similar global matching and retrieval performance, with differences within one point on most metrics (Appendix B), indicating that our results are not sensitive to the specific number of semantic families. We adopt K39 as our main taxonomy for its modestly stronger spatial localization and broader semantic coverage. 3.2 FAMILY -C ONSTRAINED PROTOTYPE EVIDENCE

Training and evaluation. We train ProtoLIP on Itemized-CC0.3M (Lyu et al., 2026) with a frozen ItemizedCLIP backbone, optimizing only the prototype evidence layer. All external benchmarks are evaluated zero-shot. We evaluate object queries on COCO (Lin et al., 2014), VOC20 (Everingham et al., 2010), and the complete ADE20K-150 validation split (Zhou et al., 2017); phrase queries on Flickr30K Entities (Plummer et al., 2015); and caption queries on COCO and Flickr30K Enti-ties. Since COCO does not provide phrase-to-box links, we construct a caption-linked object-query benchmark by matching explicit category mentions in captions to COCO object categories and using all annotated boxes of the matched category as the target region. All methods use the same fixed manifest. Details are in Appendix G. 

Baselines and metrics. We compare against the frozen ItemizedCLIP backbone, released VLMs, training-free localization methods, and spatially supervised grounding models (Figure 6). ## 4 RESULTS 

Our evaluation examines whether ProtoLIP improves evidence separation across query granularities and whether these gains generalize across datasets and vision–language backbones. We first evaluate evidence disentanglement from object-level to full-caption queries (Section 4.1), and then show how the same prototype evidence exactly decomposes the model’s predictions (Section 4.2). We next test transfer to frozen public backbones and compare against public localization and supervised grounding methods (Section 4.3), followed by component ablations (Section 4.4). Additional results and controlled comparisons are provided in the appendix. 4.1 EVIDENCE DISENTANGLEMENT ACROSS QUERY GRANULARITIES 

We first test whether ProtoLIP can mitigate the object-level evidence entanglement illustrated in Figures 1 and 3 on OOD benchmarks. As shown in Figure 4, ProtoLIP improves both Pointing and Energy on VOC20 object queries and ADE20K class queries. Gains on ADE20K are more mod-est, consistent with its broader long-tail vocabulary and greater semantic mismatch with the 0.3M text-only training corpus. Nevertheless, ProtoLIP also improves AUC and BAcc on both bench-marks, showing that the localization gains are accompanied by stronger matching performance. The same prototype pathway that separates object-level evidence also exactly decomposes the matching score of a complete sentence query. Figure 5 illustrates this for the caption “a gray and white kitten sitting in a bathroom sink.” The complete text representation evaluates the image-specific evidence of all eligible prototypes through sk(I, T ), and each prototype contributes 

qk(I, T ) = αk(T )sk(I, T ) to the final score. Aggregating these prototype contributions within se-mantic families reveals distinct support from the animal and indoor-scene evidence, and the resulting family contributions sum exactly to the final prototype-mediated matching score.

We ablate ProtoLIP on COCO to isolate the effects of prototype mediation, semantic organization, and spatial regularization. As shown in Table 3, a flat prototype layer already improves object-level localization, while semantic family organization provides the largest additional gain. The area and overlap constraints further improve evidence quality by discouraging diffuse responses and excessive sharing between active families, with their combination achieving the strongest overall Pointing and Energy. All prototype variants use the same parameter budget, adding only 2.23M trainable parameters (1.48% of the frozen backbone). Full parameter counts are provided in Appendix D. 

## 5 CONCLUSION 

We show that increasingly fine-grained text conditioning does not necessarily yield correspondingly precise object-level evidence. We introduce ProtoLIP, a lightweight prototype-mediated evidence layer that improves query-specific localization and separation without spatial supervision or back-bone retraining. Overall, the fallback mechanism prevents unmatched queries from being silently discarded or as-signed to arbitrary semantic families.

## G EVALUATION PROTOCOL AND IMPLEMENTATION DETAILS 

Prototype layer architecture. In our implementation, ϕθ is a gated two-layer residual MLP with hidden width d/ 2, GELU activation, residual scale γ = 0 .25 , and final ℓ2 normalization. We opti-mize the prototype evidence layer with AdamW for 6,000 steps using a learning rate of 1.5 × 10 −3

and weight decay of 10 −3. The effective batch size is 1,024. Distributed runs use two NVIDIA GPUs with 256 samples per GPU and two gradient-accumulation steps; single-GPU sensitivity ex-periments use a batch size of 256 with four accumulation steps. Unless otherwise specified, reported ProtoLIP results are averaged over three independent training runs with random seeds 1713, 1714, and 1715. The prototype and matching temperatures are set to 0.04 and 0.05 , respectively. Image patches are pooled with a 2 × 2 region size. We set λarea = 1 .5, τa = 0 .14 , λoverlap = 0 .25 , and τo = 0 .20 .Gradient checkpointing and chunked score computation are used to reduce memory consumption without changing the model or objective. The frozen vision–language backbone is shared across all variants, and no KD, teacher branch, or inference-time score fusion is used for the main model. 

External baseline checkpoints. We evaluate the public openmmlab-community/ mm_grounding_dino_large_all checkpoint, whose training corpus includes GoldG, V3Det, COCO2017, LVIS-v1, COCO2014, GRIT, RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg, and gRefCOCO. NACLIP uses CLIP-B/16, and FLAIR uses the released merged-30M checkpoint. 

COCO caption-linked object queries. COCO does not provide phrase–box correspondences, so we construct a fixed caption-linked object-query manifest from validation captions. Before model evaluation, we define a boundary-aware alias list for COCO instance categories, e.g., bike for bicycle 

and woman /man for person , and apply deterministic lexical matching to each caption. We retain only matched categories with at least one ground-truth instance box in the image, discarding unmatched, non-localizable, or unannotated mentions. If multiple instances of a matched category are present, their boxes form a union target: Pointing is correct when the top evidence patch falls inside any instance box, and Energy is the evidence mass within their union. The resulting manifest contains 681 object queries over 557 images and is shared by all methods. 17
