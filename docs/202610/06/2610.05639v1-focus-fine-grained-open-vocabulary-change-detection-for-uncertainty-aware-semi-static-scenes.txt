# FOCUS: Fine-Grained Open-Vocabulary Change Detection for Uncertainty-Aware Semi-Static Scenes 

Can Xu 1, Mingfeng Yuan 1, Mahan Mohammadi 1, and Steven L. Waslander 1

Abstract — Autonomous robots operating over long periods must keep their environmental memory up to date as the world changes between visits. In semi-static environments, an object may be replaced in place by a different but geometrically and semantically similar instance, making the identity change difficult to detect from geometry or coarse semantics alone. We present FOCUS , an uncertainty-aware framework for object-level change detection and map maintenance. We formulate semi-static memory maintenance as probabilistic inference that fuses geometric likelihood with appearance likelihood rendered from a 3D Gaussian map. A recursive three-state estimator maintains whether each mapped object is PERSISTED ,

> REPLACED

, or REMOVED . To account for imperfect 3DGS rendering, we model the rendered appearance evidence proba-bilistically rather than using it as a direct change score, with the model parameters automatically calibrated from a change-free replay of the initial mapping session. We evaluate our method on a new Isaac Sim warehouse benchmark with ambiguous in-place replacements and on the real-world TorWIC dataset. It improves object-level replacement F1 from 0.20 to 0.84 over a probabilistic baseline and transfers to real-world data without manual parameter retuning. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The rapid advance of foundation models in recent years has greatly expanded robots’ ability to recognize unseen ob-jects and understand open-ended environments. This progress is pushing robotics beyond short-horizon perception and reactive behavior toward long-term autonomy, where robots must operate reliably over extended periods as the world evolves. A key step toward this goal is persistent embodied intelligence: the ability to maintain an up-to-date memory of the environment, understand how it changes, and use that knowledge to adapt future decisions. Such capability is essential for warehouse inventory auditing, long-term crop monitoring, construction-site inspection, and household ser-vice robots, where objects may be added, removed, moved, replaced, or undergo meaningful changes in appearance or state over time. These settings are naturally semi-static :most of the environment remains stable, while a small but important subset undergoes persistent change. Reliable operation in such environments therefore requires robots to distinguish genuine changes from viewpoint variation, sensing noise, transient observations, and subtle differences between visually similar objects. Recent open-vocabulary scene representations and scene graphs have enabled robots to build increasingly rich object-centric memories of their environments [1], [2].

Existing semi-static mapping methods have begun to address this problem through geometric and probabilistic consistency estimation [3], [4]. However, these methods are primarily designed to detect coarse scene changes rather than fine-grained changes in object identity or state. POV-SLAM [5], for example, probabilistically esti-mates whether mapped objects remain consistent with cur-rent observations, but assumes prior semantic knowledge of mapped objects and models object state largely through geometric consistency. Khronos [4] similarly reasons about long-term scene evolution using geometric visibility and free-space evidence to verify whether previously observed structures remain present. These approaches are effective when changes produce a clear geometric signature, such as object addition, removal, or displacement. They become much more ambiguous under in-place replacement , where an object is replaced by another instance with nearly identical geometry and coarse semantics. For example, two cardboard boxes may occupy the same volume and share the same category while differing in a fragile label, plastic wrapping, or an open/closed state. Such differences may generate little geometric residual, yet can be operationally important for applications such as warehouse inventory reconciliation or long-term crop-condition monitoring. Appearance can be used to augment geometric change detection, but appearance evidence is itself uncertain. Dense visual features can distinguish objects that share similar geometry and semantics, and recent methods exploit im-age descriptors such as DINO-based features for associa-tion across observations [6]. However, pairwise matching usually requires sufficiently overlapping views, which may not exist across long-term revisits. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) provides an attractive alternative by storing a view-conditioned appearance model that can render the expected appearance of a mapped object from the current camera pose [7], [8]. This enables direct comparison between the current observation and the expected appearance without requiring a co-visible reference image. However, rendered appearance can be unreliable due to limited object visi-bility, incomplete reconstruction, floaters, pose errors, and illumination changes. A single-frame appearance residual is therefore insufficient for reliable change detection. Simply accumulating more observations is also problematic: along patrol-style trajectories, robots often observe an object from highly similar viewpoints over consecutive frames, causing correlated evidence to be repeatedly counted and resulting in overconfident decisions. 

We therefore formulate semi-static memory mainte-arXiv:2610.05639v1 [cs.RO] 5 Oct 2026 nance as uncertainty-aware probabilistic inference over object state. For each mapped object, our system maintains a recursive three-state belief over PERSISTED , REPLACED ,and REMOVED . Geometry and appearance provide comple-mentary evidence: geometric cues are effective for detecting disappearance and newly occupied space, while rendered appearance provides the fine-grained signal needed to dis-tinguish a persisted object from an in-place replacement. To make this appearance evidence reliable, we maintain an object-specific appearance baseline together with its uncer-tainty, compensate for frame-level appearance shifts, sup-press unstable rendered evidence, and reduce the influence of temporally correlated observations. The resulting geometry and appearance likelihoods are fused in a recursive Bayesian estimator whose parameters are calibrated from a change-free replay of the reference session. In this way, our framework shifts semi-static memory maintenance from asking only 

“has the mapped geometry changed?” toward the finer question “is this still the same object?” 

We summarize our contributions as follows: 1) We introduce an uncertainty-aware 3DGS appearance model for fine-grained object change detection, ac-counting for appearance variation and unreliable ren-dered evidence. 2) We formulate object-level memory maintenance as a three-state recursive Bayesian estimator over PER -

> SISTED

, REPLACED , and REMOVED , fusing comple-mentary geometric and appearance evidence while accounting for unstable and correlated observations. 3) We introduce a multi-session warehouse benchmark for fine-grained semi-static changes, with challenging in-place replacements between geometrically and seman-tically similar objects and a complete set of object-level change annotations. II. R ELATED WORK 

A. Long-Term Object-Level Map Maintenance 

Long-term map maintenance has traditionally relied on geometric inconsistency across revisits. TSDF- and SDF-based methods detect persistent changes from reconstruction differences [9], [10], while Khronos [4] uses geometric and free-space evidence to infer long-term object persistence. Such methods are effective for additions, removals, and displacement, but can be ambiguous for in-place replacement between geometrically similar objects. To provide additional cues beyond geometry, recent object-centric maps incorpo-rate semantic features and language descriptions [1], [2], [8], [11]. However, coarse semantics may still be insufficient to distinguish visually similar instances. We instead target fine-grained object identity changes by combining geometry with uncertainty-aware, view-conditioned appearance evidence. 

B. Appearance-Based Scene Change Detection 

Appearance provides complementary evidence for cross-session change detection. Recent 3DGS-based methods ex-ploit novel-view rendering to compare a reference scene with current observations. 3DGS-CD [12] detects 2D object-level changes from rendered references and fuses them across views to recover 3D change masks and object transforma-tions, while Galappaththige et al. [13] extend this setting to online scene change detection with multi-view fusion and change-guided 3DGS updates. DynamicGSG [7] is par-ticularly related to our setting: it renders visible mapped objects, compares them with current observations using SSIM, and removes objects whose similarity falls below a fixed threshold, treating them as moved or substituted. In contrast, we treat rendered appearance as an uncertain identity observation rather than a hard change decision, and recursively fuse it with geometric evidence to distinguish 

> PERSISTED

, REPLACED , and REMOVED objects. 

C. Probabilistic Object-State Estimation 

Change evidence from individual observations can be un-reliable due to sensor noise, localization error, and imperfect segmentation. Persistence filtering therefore accumulates ev-idence over time rather than making single-frame decisions. Hashemifar and Dantu [14] incorporate feature-persistence reasoning into visual SLAM, while POCD [3] recursively estimates object stationarity from geometric and semantic evidence. Bogenberger et al. [15], referred to as Perceive-Semantix, incorporate geometric and semantic similarity for persistent object-instance tracking. Building on this prob-abilistic formulation, we explicitly distinguish PERSISTED ,

> REPLACED

, and REMOVED states and recursively fuse ge-ometry with uncertainty-aware appearance evidence. 

D. Datasets for Semi-Static Change Detection 

Several datasets support evaluation in changing indoor environments. TorWIC [3] provides multi-session warehouse sequences with object additions, removals, translations, and rotations for map-maintenance evaluation. 3RScan [16] con-tains repeated scans of indoor scenes with dense instance annotations, cross-session correspondences, and object trans-formations, primarily targeting object re-localization. However, these datasets do not directly target fine-grained object-state estimation under ambiguous in-place replace-ment. Our benchmark therefore introduces geometrically and semantically similar replacements with per-object change states and globally consistent instance identities across ses-sions. III. A. Problem Formulation and Assumptions 

In this work, we consider object-level change detection and semantic map maintenance across multiple traversals of a semi-static environment. Specifically, the environment remains static during each visit, and object changes only occur when the robot is away. We construct an object-level reference map during the first traversal, capturing scene geometry, semantics, object identity, and appearance information. During each revisit, we detect newly appeared objects and recursively update the state belief for each mapped object to determine whether its state has changed, updating the reference map accordingly. B. System Pipeline Overview 

As shown in Fig. 1, our system has two stages: building a reference map MR in the initial traversal, and running change detection and maintenance in later revisits. The input is a sequence of aligned RGB-D frames F = {Ft}t=1 ...T 

with their camera-to-world poses T = {T W C t }t=1 ...T . In the first stage, we incrementally build MR from scratch, consisting of a TSDF voxel map V for geometry and a Gaussian Splatting model G for appearance. At the same time, we maintain an object library O = {O0, . . . , O n}.After construction, the reference recording is replayed on the reference map to calibrate the filter without labels. In the second stage, we monitor the state of every mapped object against MR online. Specifically, each object Oi

carries a three-state belief bi(S), Eq. (1), recursively updated as observations accumulate. Then, decisions are applied to the map during the revisit. 

C. Reference Map Construction 

Building on OmniMap [8], we construct and maintain a geometry TSDF voxel map V and an appearance Gaussian Splatting model G in parallel. Objects are detected and seg-mented by an open-vocabulary front-end: we use DART [17], built on SAM3 [18], to obtain instance masks in real time, and then apply TAP [19] to generate a refined segmentation mask with a description caption for each candidate. The caption is encoded into a semantic embedding by the SBERT encoder. We then project these 2D masks into 3D voxels using the depth. Following OmniMap, we adopt probabilistic voxel association. To be more specific, each voxel maintains several candidate instance IDs with their vote counts. Given a new mask, we compute a weighted sum of geometric similarity and semantic similarity for each candidate, and determine whether the mask is associated to an existing object or initializes a new one. After several confirmed observations, objects are added to the object library O and kept in the map. During Gaussian Splatting training, we select keyframes by accumulated translation and rotation rather than at a fixed frame interval. On patrol-style trajectories, the robot speed varies substantially, so selecting keyframes by frame count makes the spatial density of the appearance reference vary with speed, resulting in uneven rendering quality. The resulting reference map MR = {V , G, O} renders the reference scene from an arbitrary camera view, providing the geometric and appearance reference for each later revisit.

2) Evaluation Metrics: We evaluate change-detection per-formance at two levels: voxel-level and object-level. At the voxel level, a ground-truth surface voxel counts as changed if the map segment covering it is declared changed by the method, and as a false positive if it belongs to an unchanged object. The voxel level does not require object-level instance matching and mainly focuses on spatial correctness. At the object level, the unit is the annotated physical object. Each detection is assigned to the ground-truth object containing the majority of its voxels, or treated as unmatched if it does not overlap any evaluable object. Both unmatched detections and detections assigned to unchanged objects are counted as false positives. 

B. Isaac Sim Warehouse Dataset 

Each warehouse provides four sequences. A reference traversal is denoted t0, two revisits with replacement and removal are denoted trp and trm , and a static revisit tstatic , in which nothing changes, is used to measure false detections. Table I reports the results under the two-level protocol. We first compare all methods on tstatic . Since nothing changes, every detection is a false positive. The object-level false-positive rate of Perceive-Semantix is 14.6% on the large warehouse and 33.8% on the small one, and DynamicGSG flags 55.9% of the unchanged objects on the small ware-house, since a single-frame SSIM drop is enough for it to delete an object. Ours is 3.2% and 10.3%. For our method this also shows that the threshold θ, calibrated by replaying 

t0, transfers to the different trajectory of tstatic . Fig. 3 illustrates how evidence accumulates for a box replaced by one of identical size but with a different label. On changed revisits our method also shows high recall in all cases. It detects all changed objects in the small ware-house and more than 68% of them in the large warehouse, while Perceive-Semantix’s recall does not exceed 0.23 in any case, and DynamicGSG reaches at most 0.64 while flagging more than half of the unchanged objects. In particular, the gap is widest on replacement, the case our appearance model is designed for. For Perceive-Semantix, replacement is harder than removal, with a voxel-level F1 of 0.13 on replacement against 0.18 on removal in the small warehouse. For our method the two cases are comparable, with 0.76 against 0.67.

Perceive-Semantix suffers mainly from association. Ware-house assets are packed tightly, and its association merges new detections into old objects 591 times on the large warehouse, which also explains its high false-positive rate on tstatic , as shown in Fig. 4. Our method has a failure mode of its own. On the large-warehouse removal revisit the object-level F1 drops to 0.53, and in both scenes the false-positive rate on removal is higher than on replacement. We attribute this to online map maintenance. Once an object is removed, it exposes surfaces of neighbouring objects that were never observed during reference mapping, whose rendered appearance then mismatches the observation and leads them to be flagged as changed. E. Computation Time 

Our system runs on an RTX 5090. Reference mapping is performed once per map, taking 1.8 s per frame on the 1280 ×720 warehouse sequences and 0.6 s on the 640 ×360 TorWIC sequences plus a calibration replay. Each revisit costs 0.73 s per frame on the warehouses and 0.27 s on TorWIC, dominated by segmentation, rendering, and TSDF integration, while the change filter itself takes 0.2 ms per object observation. Perceive-Semantix costs 1.2 s and 0.2 s, and DynamicGSG 3.3 s and 2.3 s at 640 ×360. VII. C ONCLUSIONS 

We presented FOCUS, an uncertainty-aware framework for object-level change detection and map maintenance in semi-static environments. FOCUS combines geometric consistency with view-conditioned appearance from a 3D Gaussian map and recursively estimates whether each ob-ject is PERSISTED , REPLACED , or REMOVED . By mod-eling appearance uncertainty and correlated observations, the method detects ambiguous in-place replacements that are difficult to identify from geometry or coarse semantics alone. Experiments on simulated warehouses and the real-world TorWIC dataset demonstrate improved change detec-tion and map maintenance, highlighting the importance of uncertainty-aware appearance reasoning for persistent object-centric memory.
