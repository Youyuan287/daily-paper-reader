# MaRO-GS: Mask-Robust Object-Centric Gaussian Splatting from Inconsistent Multi-view Masks 

Eunji Kim ∗, Gahyeon Kim ∗, Gianella Cravioto, Dong-hun Lee, Chaewon Moon, Chae-yeong Song, and Sang-hyo Park †

School of Computer Science and Engineering Kyungpook National University, Daegu, Republic of Korea 

{now0104, hwang190190, gianellacrv9, hy05205, moonchaewon2, yeongsong, s.park}@knu.ac.kr https://eunjikim02.github.io/marogs/ 

Abstract. We address the challenge of accurate 3D object reconstruc-tion from multi-view images in Gaussian Splatting. Existing object-level 3DGS methods reconstruct the entire scene rather than directly optimiz-ing the target object, even when only the target object is needed, which incurs substantial computational overhead. They also rely on 2D segmen-tation masks to associate Gaussians with objects, but these masks are often inconsistent across views. Such inconsistencies corrupt Gaussian optimization and produce incorrectly supervised Gaussians that degrade object reconstruction fidelity. To overcome these limitations, we pro-pose MaRO-GS, a 3DGS framework that directly optimizes target-object Gaussians from object-masked multi-view images and remains robust to inconsistent supervision. For reliable supervision, mask-reliability view filtering excludes unreliable views. Object-supported Gaussian density control suppresses Gaussians irrelevant to the target object and prevents background densification, while Silhouette-aligned Object Loss main-tains object-focused optimization. Extensive experiments across diverse datasets demonstrate that MaRO-GS improves PSNR, segmentation ac-curacy, and computational efficiency, with the largest PSNR gain of 2.05 dB on the small-object LERF-Mask dataset. 

Keywords: 3D Gaussian Splatting, Object-centric Reconstruction, Mask Supervision

2 Related Work 

2.1 Scene-level Reconstruction in 3DGS 

Recent 3DGS [20] research has substantially improved scene-level reconstruc-tion fidelity through enriched Gaussian representations and adaptive density control [4, 21, 28, 42, 44]. Other studies have instead focused on improving com-putational efficiency through pruning and compression techniques [9, 10, 12–14, 31,32,34]. However, these scene-level 3DGS methods are designed for entire scene reconstruction and do not account for individual objects within the scene. 

2.2 Object-level Reconstruction in 3DGS 

Several methods [29, 35, 41, 46, 48] have extended 3DGS to support object-level reconstruction. For example, ObjectGS [48] introduces object-aware anchors, COB-GS [46] and Trace3D [35] address boundary ambiguity through Gaussian refinement, and BEA-GS [29] improves object extraction via boundary and occu-pancy losses. However, these methods perform scene-level Gaussian optimization prior to object extraction, which results in unnecessary computational overhead. Since scene-level reconstruction optimizes Gaussian primitives for the entire scene, Gaussians near object boundaries receive supervision from both object and background regions. As a result, ambiguous Gaussians emerge, which limits ob-ject extraction quality despite various boundary refinement strategies [29,35,46]. Unlike these approaches, MaRO-GS directly reconstructs the target object from object-masked multi-view images and applies object-supported Gaussian density control to better constrain Gaussian optimization around the target object. 4 E. 2.3 2D Mask Supervision in 3DGS 

Recent object-aware methods have leveraged 2D masks predicted by SAM [22] and lifted them into 3D representations [15–17, 36, 40, 41]. This enables appli-cations such as object extraction, segmentation, and scene editing in 3DGS. Although SAM provides powerful segmentation capabilities, it produces incon-sistent masks across views for the same object. These inconsistencies manifest in both object boundaries [29, 35, 46] and mask granularity [7]. They introduce conflicting supervision signals into the reconstruction process. As a result, object-centric Gaussian optimization becomes unreliable and reconstruction quality de-grades. To address this, Gaussian Grouping [41] and ObjectGS [48] adopt dif-ferent strategies to establish cross-view mask consistency. Specifically, Gaussian Grouping employs the DEVA [6] tracker for cross-view object ID tracking, while ObjectGS uses majority voting to assign object IDs. In both cases, the focus remains on cross-view object ID consistency rather than assessing the reliabil-ity of individual 2D mask predictions. Consequently, inaccurate masks can still be used as supervision during optimization. Trace3D [35] also addresses multi-view mask inconsistency via GIT-based instance weight tracing. However, as it is designed for multi-object segmentation within a fully reconstructed scene, its consistency enforcement operates across all objects simultaneously rather than explicitly identifying and filtering low-quality masks for a target object. Despite their differences, these methods do not explicitly filter inconsistent masks to ensure clean supervision for object-centric reconstruction. 3 Methods 

We propose MaRO-GS (Ma sk-Robust Object-centric Gaussian Splatting), il-lustrated in Fig. 2. Our method first formalizes the object-centric reconstruction task (Sec. 3.1). To ensure reliable optimization under imperfect mask supervi-sion, we introduce mask-reliability view filtering , which enforces geometric consis-tency across training views (Sec. 3.2). We further propose object-supported Gaus-sian density control to suppress background Gaussians and concentrate density around target objects (Sec. 3.3). Finally, we design a Silhouette-aligned object loss function that prioritizes object regions during optimization (Sec. 3.4). This unified design transforms conventional scene-level 3DGS into an object-centric reconstruction pipeline for robust and high-fidelity results under inconsistent mask supervision. 

3.1 Object-centric 3D Gaussian Splatting 

We formalize object-centric 3DGS for accurate and efficient reconstruction of a target object by directly performing Gaussian optimization on object-masked observations, without reconstructing the entire scene. Specifically, given an RGB image I and binary object mask M , we construct an object-masked observation: 

˜I = I ⊙ M,  (1) where ⊙ denotes element-wise multiplication. This masking operation removes irrelevant scene regions and focuses the optimization on the target object. How-ever, simply applying object masks is insufficient, as optimization becomes di-rectly dependent on their quality and consistency. When masks are inaccurate or inconsistent across views, the same 3D region may be supervised as foreground in one view and background in another, which corrupts Gaussian optimization and adaptive density control. Therefore, object-centric 3DGS requires robust optimization under imperfect mask supervision, which motivates the designs in the following sections. 

3.2 Mask-Reliability View Filtering 

Since MaRO-GS directly optimizes an object-centric Gaussian representation from masked observations, the reliability of each object mask directly determines the quality of supervision. However, segmentation models such as SAM [22] often produce view-dependent mask errors, including missing object regions, boundary inaccuracies, and background inclusions. As shown in Fig. 3, even a small number of erroneous masks can disturb Gaussian optimization and lead to rendering artifacts in multiple views. This indicates that mask errors are not isolated 2D supervision noise, but can propagate through the shared 3D Gaussian representation. We therefore propose mask-reliability view filtering ,which identifies unreliable views and excludes them from subsequent training. 3.3 Object-supported Gaussian Density Control 

While mask-reliability view filtering improves the reliability of view-level su-pervision, object-centric reconstruction also requires density-level control over the Gaussian representation. The Gaussian distribution should be allocated and maintained in regions that receive consistent target-object support across views. Without this constraint, Gaussians near object boundaries or residual back-ground regions can persist during optimization and may be further amplified by adaptive densification. These unsupported Gaussians degrade object com-pactness and introduce residual background artifacts. To address this issue, we propose object-supported Gaussian density control , which refines the Gaussian distribution through two complementary operations: Mask-guided Pruning and Densification and Appearance-guided Local Gaussian Merging .

Mask-Guided Pruning and Densification. To remove such unsupported primitives, we measure how consistently each Gaussian is supported by object masks across views. Appearance-guided Local Gaussian Merging. While mask-guided prun-ing removes background artifacts, redundant and overlapping Gaussians can still accumulate on the foreground object. To improve object-centric reconstruction fidelity, we introduce an appearance-guided local Gaussian merging strategy that consolidates nearby primitives with similar appearance. For each Gaus-sian, neighboring primitives are identified within a spatial radius proportional to its scale. Among these neighboring primitives, Gaussians with similar color are selected, and merging is performed when the number of candidates exceeds a predefined threshold. The merged Gaussian replaces the group by averaging the geometric and appearance attributes—position, scale, rotation, and color—while accumulating opacity values to preserve the overall density of the region.

Our method follows the default 3DGS [20] configuration, except that the den-sification gradient threshold is increased from 0.0002 to 0.0003. For datasets requiring generated masks, we use EfficientViT-SAM [47] as the segmentation model, but our method is not tied to a specific mask generator; additional results with other mask generators are provided in the supplementary material. All ex-periments are conducted on a single NVIDIA A6000 GPU and ablation studies are performed on a single NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti Super GPU. 4.4 Additional Evaluation for Object-centric Reconstruction 

Existing 3DGS benchmarks are primarily designed for reconstruction under con-sistent supervision and therefore cannot adequately measure robustness under multi-view inconsistencies. Moreover, conventional metrics may not capture per-ceptually meaningful visual quality differences when the target object occupies only a small image region, as shown in Fig. 7. To address these limitations, we conduct two additional evaluations for object-centric scenarios: (1) Robustness Evaluation under Mask Errors , which measures reconstruction robustness under varying levels of mask inconsistency, and (2) Object-centric Viewpoint Evalua-tion , which assesses perceptual fidelity from object-centric viewpoints.

6 Limitations 

Although MaRO-GS improves robustness under inconsistent mask supervision, severe segmentation failures can still degrade reconstruction quality. The Medium and Hard DTU subsets are limited to 3 and 4 scenes, respectively, and conser-vative filtering achieves only 4.6% recall on Hard, so the overall Hard-case im-provement cannot be attributed to view filtering alone. Filtering also depends on sufficiently localized Gaussian support during optimization. Further analyses and failure cases are provided in the supplementary material. 

7 Conclusion 

We present MaRO-GS for object-centric 3D Gaussian Splatting under inconsis-tent mask supervision. MaRO-GS combines mask-reliability view filtering with object-supported Gaussian density control to exclude unreliable supervision and suppress Gaussians irrelevant to the target object. Experiments demonstrate im-proved PSNR and robustness, complemented by object-centric and mask-error robustness evaluations.

A Implementation Details 

We follow the default 3DGS optimization settings unless otherwise specified. All models are trained for 30K iterations with spherical harmonics degree 3. We use the default learning rates for Gaussian position, feature, opacity, scaling, and rotation parameters, and set the DSSIM weight to λdssim = 0 .2. For the 

silhouette-aligned object loss , we set the background weight to γ = 0 .01 in all experiments. For the proposed modules, we use the following configuration in all experiments. Mask-reliability view filtering is enabled by default. Geometric view consistency is performed from iteration 1K to 5K at 2K-iteration intervals, using up to 20 pose-neighboring views selected by camera-center distance and the top-3 weighted similarities for each view score. The adaptive largest-gap threshold is accepted only when the low-score cluster occupies at most 25% of the views and the cluster separation is larger than 0.5. Region-based view validation is applied once at iteration 1.8K with a hit-ratio threshold of 0.05. For object-supported Gaussian density control , mask-guided pruning is performed at iterations 1K, 3K, and 5K with pruning coefficient k = 0 .5, maximum pruning threshold 0.05, and prune ratio 1.0. C.7 Silhouette-aligned Object Loss 

We analyze the effect of the background loss weight γ, which controls the con-tribution of background regions during training. When γ = 0 , the model is opti-mized using only the masked object region, completely ignoring the background. Although this enforces strong object-centric supervision, it can reduce train-ing stability because surrounding scene context is not considered. Introducing a small background weight improves reconstruction stability while still emphasiz-ing the target object. As shown in Fig. 20, γ = 0 .01 maintains high PSNR and low LPIPS while preserving efficient rendering time. In contrast, when γ becomes too large ( γ = 0 .1), the training objective becomes dominated by background regions, which weakens the object-centric constraint and degrades reconstruction quality. Based on these observations, we select γ = 0 .01 as the default setting since it provides the best trade-off between object-focused supervision and stable scene optimization. E.3 Comparison with LongSplat 

Table 23 summarizes the differences in covisibility usage between LongSplat [26] and MaRO-GS. While both methods utilize Gaussian visibility to measure view-to-view relationships, they serve fundamentally different purposes in their re-spective pipelines. LongSplat computes a binary IoU of visible-Gaussian sets between frame pairs, and uses it to decide which nearby frames should be jointly optimized within a visibility-adapted local window, ensuring reliable multi-view supervision during incremental pose and scene optimization. In contrast, MaRO-GS leverages Gaussian visibility IoU across reference views as a reliability met-ric for view consistency. By evaluating the overlap between a target training view and its geometrically related reference views, MaRO-GS effectively dis-cards geometrically inconsistent views that might otherwise introduce artifacts. This mechanism ensures robust object-centric reconstruction, particularly when dealing with noisy or inconsistent mask supervision that commonly arises from automated segmentation models such as SAM.

F.2 Failure Cases of Existing Methods 

In the kitchen scene on Mip-NeRF 360 dataset, ObjectGS [48] failed to recon-struct any visible object when this scene was used as input, as shown in Fig. 24. ObjectGS is designed to optimize object-centric Gaussians based on object segmentation priors. When the object region is small or ambiguous, the method may fail to initialize reliable object Gaussians, which can lead to an empty re-construction. PUP 3D-GS [14] showed stable behavior on Mip-NeRF 360 and LERF-Mask but exhibited a failure case on the DTU dataset. In several DTU scenes, the method pruned most Gaussians in the scene during training. Since DTU contains many views with inconsistent geometry and background clutter, the pruning strategy can incorrectly suppress Gaussians that correspond to valid object structures. This result indicates that pruning-based acceleration methods may become unstable when the importance of scene elements cannot be reliably estimated. Fig. 25 shows that even in the few scenes where training successfully completes, the reconstructed results remain inaccurate.
