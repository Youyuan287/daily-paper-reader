Abstract 

Mesh extraction from 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) aims to endow 3D Gaussians with accurate geometric structures, en-abling explicit and precise 3D occupancy. However, existing methods primarily focus on scene-level mesh extraction, mak-ing them unable to represent object-level occupancy and often resulting in non-watertight surfaces. To overcome these limita-tions, we propose MEGA (Mesh Extraction from GAussians), a “segment-then-mesh” framework for extracting object-level, watertight meshes from complex 3DGS scenes. At the core of MEGA are Spatial Visual Distillation (SVD) and a mask-guided neural surface reconstruction module. SVD treats the 3DGS model as a teacher, sampling diverse camera poses and rendering the corresponding views of each segmented object. These observations are then used to train a mesh reconstruc-tion model through photometric supervision. Extensive ex-periments on several widely used benchmarks demonstrate that MEGA achieves state-of-the-art performance in recov-ering accurate object-level 3D occupancy. Moreover, MEGA enables complex physical interactions by combining high-quality object-level meshes for geometric occupancy with 3DGS representations for photorealistic rendering. 

## 1. Introduction 

The goal of this work is to extract object-level meshes from complex 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) (Kerbl et al. 2023) scenes, thereby providing explicit and accurate object-level 3D occupancy. As one of the most prominent 3D representa-tions in recent years, 3DGS faces a fundamental limitation: it can be seen, but not touched . Although 3DGS explicitly rep-resents a scene using Gaussian primitives, it does not encode coherent surface geometry. Moreover, the anisotropic nature of these primitives means that they do not necessarily lie on the true surfaces of objects. Consequently, 3DGS lacks the geometric structure required for reliable physical interaction, posing a major obstacle to its application in physical-world simulation. Numerous methods (Guédon and Lepetit 2024; Huang et al. 2024; Chen et al. 2024; Guédon et al. 2025; Chang et al. 2026; Held et al. 2025, 2026) have attempted to reconstruct meshes from 3DGS, with the goal of providing explicit 3D occupancy and making 3DGS representations

“touchable.” However, these methods typically suffer from two major limitations. First, they primarily extract scene-level meshes, which are poorly suited to object-level inter-action and manipulation. Second, the resulting meshes are often non-watertight and therefore fail to provide complete and reliable 3D occupancy. 

Why, then, is object-level occupancy essential for 3DGS? 

The answer lies in the need to faithfully model the physical world. Although 3DGS excels at representing visual appear-ance, it lacks the explicit object-level geometry required for physical interaction. Scene-level geometry alone is insuf-ficient: practical applications require individual objects to be localized, manipulated, grasped, and simulated indepen-dently. Object-level meshes therefore provide the missing geometric interface between photorealistic 3DGS represen-tations and physically interactive environments. To bridge this gap, we propose MEGA , a framework for extracting object-level meshes from 3DGS scenes. MEGA not only extracts watertight meshes for individual objects but also maintains accurate geometric and coordinate alignment between the extracted meshes and the underlying 3D Gaus-sians. It thus provides effective object-level 3D occupancy while supporting richer interactions and manipulations, as illustrated in Fig. 1. At the core of MEGA is Spatial Visual Distillation (SVD) . Given a target object, SVD constructs a carefully designed array of virtual cameras and renders the segmented object from diverse viewpoints. These renderings serve as teacher signals for training a neural mesh recon-struction module, ultimately producing an object-level mesh aligned with the coordinate system of the original 3DGS scene. In summary, our main contributions are as follows: 

• We propose MEGA , an object-level mesh extraction framework for 3DGS that provides accurate object-level 3D occupancy in complex scenes and supports rich object-level interactions and manipulations. 

• We introduce Spatial Visual Distillation , which distills multi-view visual knowledge from 3DGS into a neural mesh reconstruction model. 

• We present an early systematic exploration of object-level mesh extraction from 3DGS and establish a strong base-line for future research on physically interactive 3DGS representations. arXiv:2610.01707v1 [cs.CV] 1 Oct 2026 Joint Representation

## 2. RELATED WORKS 

2.1. 3DGS Segmentation 

3DGS segmentation partitions the Gaussian primitives in a scene into distinct subsets, each corresponding to an in-dividual object. By assigning semantic identities to other-wise appearance-oriented Gaussian primitives, segmenta-tion provides essential spatial priors for subsequent object-level mesh extraction. Existing methods can be broadly di-vided into two categories: offline methods , which require per-scene optimization, and online methods , which oper-ate in an inference-only manner. Among offline methods, SAGA (Cen et al. 2025) distills scale-gated affinity features from multi-view 2D masks and assigns semantic attributes to individual Gaussians. Click-Gaussian (Choi et al. 2024) aggregates global feature candidates to achieve consistent feature learning across views. COB-GS (Zhang et al. 2025) further introduces boundary-adaptive Gaussian splitting to resolve ambiguities near object boundaries. Despite their ef-fectiveness, these methods require additional optimization for each scene, which typically takes tens of minutes. In contrast, online methods perform segmentation with-out per-scene training. GaussianCut (Jain, Mirzaei, and Gilitschenski 2024) constructs a graph over Gaussian prim-itives and formulates segmentation as a graph-partitioning problem. SAGD (Hu et al. 2024) introduces Gaussian de-composition to alleviate jagged boundary artifacts. More recently, SAGO (Liao, Wang, and Wang 2026) introduces virtual drones for interactive 3D segmentation, achieving sub-second latency without per-scene optimization. In this work, we adopt SAGO to obtain the object-level segmenta-tion priors required by our framework. 

2.2. Mesh Extraction from 3DGS 

Numerous methods have explored mesh extraction from 3D Gaussian representations. SuGaR (Guédon and Lepetit 2024) introduces a regularization term that encourages Gaussian primitives to align with scene surfaces, followed by mesh extraction using Poisson surface reconstruction (Kazhdan, Bolitho, and Hoppe 2006). SAGD (Hu et al. 2024) applies the Quickhull algorithm (Barber, Dobkin, and Huhdanpaa 1996) to construct collision meshes for segmented objects. However, the resulting meshes are limited to coarse convex polyhedra and cannot accurately capture complex object ge-ometry. 2DGS (Huang et al. 2024) replaces 3D Gaussian primitives with 2D Gaussian disks to improve surface align-ment and subsequently extracts meshes through truncated signed distance field (TSDF) fusion. MeshSplatting (Held et al. 2025) instead adopts opaque triangles as primitives, enabling the end-to-end reconstruction of explicit meshes. Despite these advances, most existing methods, with the exception of SAGD, focus primarily on scene-level mesh extraction. Obtaining individual object meshes there-fore requires additional manual segmentation or post-processing. Moreover, the extracted meshes are frequently non-watertight, making them unsuitable for representing complete and reliable object-level 3D occupancy. Although SAGD directly produces object-level collision meshes, its convex-hull representation is too coarse to accurately de-scribe object geometry. These limitations motivate our work. In contrast to ex-isting approaches, MEGA extracts object-level, watertight, and coordinate-aligned meshes from complex 3DGS scenes, providing explicit and accurate object-level 3D occupancy. We propose MEGA , a framework for extracting object-level meshes from pretrained 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) scenes. Given a pretrained 3DGS representation Θ and a user prompt p, MEGA identifies the target object and produces both a refined object-level Gaussian representation ˆΘobj and its corresponding mesh Mobj . 3.3. Spatial Visual Distillation 

We introduce Spatial Visual Distillation (SVD) , a cross-representation distillation paradigm designed for 3D recon-struction. SVD transfers high-quality multi-view observa-tions from a teacher 3DGS representation to a student neural surface representation. This formulation combines the ren-dering quality of 3DGS with the explicit geometric modeling capability of neural surfaces, ultimately enabling the extrac-tion of an accurate and watertight object-level mesh.

All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

4.3. Qualitative Comparison 

As shown in Fig. 4, compared with existing mainstream mesh extraction methods, our MEGA produces more accurate wa-tertight meshes with well-aligned geometric structures. In contrast, although SAM3D can generate a watertight mesh from a single view, its reconstructed geometry deviates sub-stantially from that of the real object. Other splatting-based methods, PGSR, MeshSplatting and 2DGS can all produce geometrically aligned meshes, but they exhibit large holes on the bottom surface, preventing the formation of complete 3D occupancy. The non-watertight meshes may lead to severe penetration in physical simulations, as shown in Fig. 6. 

4.4. Applications 

MEGA jointly represents an object using 3DGS and a mesh, allowing physical interaction engines that previously sup-ported only meshes to operate on 3DGS objects. Our prelim-inary experiments demonstrate at least two potential appli-cations of MEGA: (1) Human–Object Interaction and (2) 

Physics Simulation . We present four examples in Fig. 5, with the first two demonstrating applications in human–object in-teraction and the latter two showcasing physics simulation. Additional examples can be found in the appendix and demo video. We hope that MEGA will provide more possibilities for the application of 3DGS in various domains.

## 5. CONCLUSION 

In this paper, we present MEGA, an object-level mesh ex-traction framework for 3DGS. By introducing Spatial Visual Distillation (SVD), MEGA uses the segmented 3DGS as a teacher model to generate multi-view camera pairs, which are then used to supervise a neural surface reconstruction network, enabling the extraction of watertight, geometry-aligned object-level meshes for effective 3D occupancy. 

Limitations. Although MEGA effectively extracts meshes for 3DGS objects, the Gaussian fitting may not perfectly align with the surface, leading to slight deviations. As a result, mi-nor mesh penetration may occur during interactions. Future work could further optimize 3DGS using the better-aligned mesh representations to address these issues. References
