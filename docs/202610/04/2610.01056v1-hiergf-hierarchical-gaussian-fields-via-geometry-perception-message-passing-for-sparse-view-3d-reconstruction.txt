# HierGF: Hierarchical Gaussian Fields via Geometry–perception Message Passing for Sparse-view 3D Reconstruction 

Bi’an Du, Zhimin Zhang, Daizong Liu, Baoquan Chen, Fellow, IEEE ,and Wei Hu, Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract —Sparse view 3D reconstruction is an important and common scenario in multimedia applications, such as augmented reality/virtual reality (AR/VR) content creation, cultural heritage digitization, and certain robotic applications, where only a limited number of randomly captured views may be available. However, sparse views contain only limited 3D information, posing two major challenges: 1) too few images are available for matching, making it difficult to build multi-view consistency; 2) insufficient view coverage leads to a lack of information in under-sampled regions, resulting in missing parts of object structure. Existing methods mostly still rely on limited reprojection errors and regularization terms, which are prone to overfitting to a single view and inconsistent appearances across views. In geometrically under-sampled regions, they often rely on heuristic density control, lacking reliable guidance and often resulting in blurring and structural holes. To address these issues, this paper proposes Hierarchical Gaussian Fields (HierGF), which revisits sparse-view reconstruction from a hierarchical geometry–perception perspective and converts limited observations into reliable self-generated supervision beyond fixed priors and heuristic density control. In particular, we transform coarse 3D geometric in-formation and additional 2D generative priors into structured pseudo-supervision through a two-stage geometry-perception backbone network, thereby enhancing multi-view consistency with very few input views. In addition, we introduce a learn-able confidence network to guide gradients toward cross-view consistent content, and a geometrically consistent densification module to improve the reconstruction of multi-view alignment and under-sampled regions. Experiments on three sparse-view reconstruction benchmarks demonstrate that HierGF improves reconstruction quality and multi-view consistency under limited observations. On the MipNeRF360 kitchen scene under the 4-view setting, HierGF completes per-scene reconstruction in 632 s and post-optimization novel-view rendering in 70 s on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU, yielding lower measured runtime than the compared optimization-based baselines. Additional quantitative comparisons on LLFF and DTU further validate the method under commonly used sparse-view protocols. 

Index Terms —Sparse-view 3D Reconstruction, Hierarchical Gaussian Fields, Geometry-perception Message Passing.

Nevertheless, they are still volumetric and remain multi-view inconsistent in severely under-constrained regions. More recently, explicit primitive-based approaches such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [16] have been adapted to sparse-view settings, where only a few images are available. This leaves open how to turn very limited observations into reliable enhanced views with strong multi-view consistency and how to optimize Gaussian primitives in a structure-aware way in under-sampled regions. To this end, this paper revisits sparse-view 3D reconstruc-tion with Hierarchical Gaussian Fields (HierGF) via geome-try–perception message passing. Through 2D-assisted percep-tion enhancement and the coarse-to-fine pipeline, we trans-form limited observational data into reliable self-generated supervised information and apply geometry-consistent density control, thus solving the two challenges mentioned above. Specifically, HierGF adopts a two-field coarse-to-fine frame-work for sparse-view 3D Gaussian reconstruction. The coarse field first provides stable geometric and color initialization. A separately optimized fine field inherits only spatial means and RGB initialization from the coarse field, while opacity, anisotropic scale, rotation, and higher-order appearance at-tributes are re-estimated using enhanced pseudo-view super-vision and geometry-consistent densification. This selective transfer retains useful coarse-stage structure without constrain-ing fine-scale refinement by the complete state of an early reconstruction. Furthermore, sparse inputs can lead to large under-sampled regions with weak image gradients, where naive density control tends to produce holes, floaters, and broken thin structures. In our framework, the optimization of the fine-scale Gaussian field not only utilizes filtered enhanced views to extend supervision beyond the original observations, but also employs a geometry-consistent densification strategy to improve the reconstruction of under-sampled regions. Specif-ically, new Gaussian primitives are inserted along estimated surface directions rather than at arbitrary locations, and a normal-weighted interpolation scheme propagates structure from well-constrained areas into nearby under-constrained ones. This allows HierGF to refine geometry and appearance in under-sampled regions while maintaining surface continuity and recovering fine details, as illustrated in Fig. 1. We group related work into three themes: 1) optimization-based per-scene sparse-view reconstruction, where our method is situated; 2) feed-forward reconstruction and 3D priors, which use a single network across multi-scenes and are reviewed mainly for conceptual context rather than as di-rect baselines; and 3) reliability-aware and structural super-vision, which motivate our confidence-guided supervision and geometry-consistent densification. 

A. Optimization-based Per-scene Sparse-view Reconstruction 

Optimization-based per-scene methods explicitly fit a 3D model for each scene. Classical space carving, voxel coloring and SfM/MVS require dense and well-calibrated views, and quickly degrade when only a few views are available [25], [26], [28], [30], [31]. NeRF [22] and its sparse-view variants [32]–[35] introduce depth and unseen-view regularization to stabilize per-scene optimization under few-shot inputs, but often depend on external predictors and still struggle in under-constrained regions [38]–[40]. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [16] has been adapted to the same setting. GaussianObject [9] exploits visual hulls with diffusion-based repair, while Pixel-GS, S2Gaussian and DropoutGS [41]–[43] refine density con-trol and capacity to alleviate overfitting and improve coverage. These pipelines still rely on fixed priors and heuristic densi-fication and ignore the reliability of synthesized supervision, which motivates our hierarchical design with 2D-assisted per-ceptual enhancement and geometry-consistent densification. B. Feed-forward Sparse-view Reconstruction and 3D Priors 

Feed-forward methods replace per-scene optimization with a single network trained across multi-scenes. Generalizable novel view synthesis (NVS) and 3DGS models such as MVSplat [44] and AnySplat [45] infer geometry or novel views in one forward pass, and recent work shows that 3D Gaussians and camera poses can be predicted directly from unconstrained views. Less3Depend [46] further synthesizes novel views from sparse, unposed inputs without explicit 3D or pose supervision, while 3D foundation models such as VGGT [47] predict cameras, depth and dense point maps for dozens of views and serve as versatile backbones for downstream 3D tasks. In parallel, diffusion-based generative pipelines provide additional 3D priors. Multi-view diffusion models like Sync-Dreamer and Zero123++ enforce cross-view consistency from very few real images [48]–[50], and PartGen combines two multi-view diffusion models for part-level 3D asset generation and reconstruction [51]. These methods excel at fast and scalable novel view synthesis while they typically do not predict an explicit 3D representation or cannot iteratively optimize scene-specific models. When meeting severely sparse or occluded input views, its geometric fidelity and editability are still relatively poor, and they do not directly target high-quality per-scene 3DGS in our setting. We therefore introduce them primarily as conceptual context and potential sources of priors, and restrict our quantitative comparisons to per-scene baselines. 

C. Reliability-aware and Structural Supervision 

Our design is also related to reliability-aware supervision and structure-aware density control. NeRF-based works use semantic features, appearance embeddings, uncertainty esti-mates and view selection to downweight ambiguous observa-tions [52]–[64], and diffusion or multi-view generative models such as DreamGaussian, SyncDreamer and Zero123++ provide strong priors for synthesized views but generally treat them as equally reliable, so local artifacts can still corrupt 3D optimiza-tion [48]–[50], [65]. In explicit 3DGS, standard splatting uses view-space residuals to drive split and grow, and SLAM3R [66] improves robustness by aligning local point maps in real time but targets online tracking rather than offline sparse-view 3DGS. These methods underline the need for robust supervision and structure-aware densification, but they do not explicitly learn per-pixel or per-view reliability for synthesized pseudo-views or tie densification to their consistency with the evolving geometry, whereas we combine 2D-assisted percep-tual enhancement, a learnable confidence module and normal-guided densification so that both supervision and Gaussian growth follow the current 3D structure. A. The Motivation 

Sparse views contain only limited 3D information and lead to two related problems. Too few images are available for matching, many regions are never seen from diverse viewpoints, reprojection losses provide little gradient, and reconstructions are easily dominated by single views with view dependent artifacts. View coverage is also poor, so large parts of the object are under-sampled and existing methods fall back on heuristic density control that often blurs missing content or produces holes, floaters and broken thin structures. HierGF addresses these issues by coupling geometry and perception in a hierarchical way. A coarse-scale Gaussian field is first estimated from sparse inputs to capture the main geom-etry, then used to render pseudo-views that are refined by a 2D-assisted perceptual supervision module with a diffusion prior and a confidence network that selects reliable pixels and views. In parallel, a geometry-consistent density completion strategy inserts new Gaussians along estimated surface directions using normal-weighted interpolation, so that a small and uneven set of views is transformed into richer supervision and under-sampled regions are filled with geometrically coherent detail. 

B. Problem Formulation and Representation 

We start with sparse reference images Xref = {xi}Ni=1 of a single object and their camera parameters Πref = {πi}Ni=1 .The goal is to learn a representation that supports photo-realistic rendering from arbitrary viewpoints with coherent geometry and cross-view appearance under few input views. We adopt 3D Gaussian Splatting [16], where each primitive is parameterized by center μj ∈ R3, rotation qj , anisotropic scale sj , opacity σj , and SH color coefficients cj . Rendering integrates projected anisotropic Gaussians along camera rays to synthesize images (and optionally depths). This explicit parameterization enables real-time rendering, and fine-grained density control, which are beneficial in sparse-view settings. In the sparse-view setting, many regions are unobserved or only weakly constrained, so Gaussian density lacks a reliable update direction. HierGF addresses this with a hierarchical ge-ometry–perception backbone: a coarse-scale field learned from sparse inputs renders additional views that are enhanced with IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON MULTIMEDIA 5

2D-assisted perceptual supervision and confidence weighting, then combined with geometry-consistent densification to refine a fine-scale field in under-sampled regions. This feedback gradually improves geometry and appearance and strengthens multi-view consistency, as illustrated in Fig. 2. 

C. Hierarchical Geometry–perception Message Passing 

Under sparse views, many regions are weakly constrained, so photometric losses give little guidance and density growth lacks direction. To avoid hallucinated details from early strong priors and unresolved holes from plain densification, we adopt a two-stage scheme: a coarse Gaussian field is first learned from sparse inputs, then refined into a fine-scale field using 2D-assisted, confidence-weighted supervision and geometry-consistent densification in under-sampled regions. The specific details are as follows: 

Coarse-grained rendering in the first stage. The first stage aims to obtain a stable geometric backbone from very sparse observations, rather than to directly chase photo-realistic de-tail. Under few-view supervision, trying to fit fine texture too early often leads to overfitting to individual images and unsta-ble density growth. We therefore focus on building a coarse but reliable Gaussian field that can be rendered consistently in the reference views and in their local neighborhoods. In this stage, the optimization emphasizes alignment of visible regions, reasonable global scale, and basic cross-view coherence, so that the resulting geometry is interpretable and can safely support subsequent perceptual enhancement and densification. Specifically, we obtain a coarse Gaussian field: 

Pc = G(xi, π i)Ni=1 . (1) Each 3D Gaussian point consists of a center position μ, a rotation quaternion q, a scale vector s, an opacity σ, and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients sh . The coarse Gaussian field is parameterized as a set of Gaussian points: 

Pc = {pi : μi, q i, s i, σ i, sh i}Pi=1 . (2) 

Fine-grained feedback in the second stage. The second stage refines the reconstruction by coupling the coarse geometry with 2D-assisted perceptual supervision. Starting from the coarse Gaussian field, we periodically render pseudo-views, enhance them with a lightweight diffusion prior to sharpen texture and harmonize shading, and feed both coarse and enhanced images into a confidence network that predicts per-pixel and per-view weights. These confidences reweight color and perceptual losses for the fine-scale field, while a geometry-consistent densification module inserts new Gaussians along estimated surface normals via normal-weighted interpolation to fill under-sampled regions in a structure-aware manner. To avoid locking in suboptimal appearance, the fine-scale field transfers only spatial means μi and RGB initializa-tion from the coarse field, while opacity, anisotropic scale, rotation, and higher-order appearance attributes are reini-tialized. As training alternates between pseudo-view gen-eration, LoRA-adapted enhancement, confidence estimation, confidence-weighted optimization, and densification, pseudo-observations track the evolving geometry and provide increas-ingly reliable multi-view supervision, which strengthens con-sistency and gradually completes previously under-constrained areas. The confidence module and geometry-consistent densifi-cation play different roles in refining the fine field: con-fidence weighting reweights the losses associated with en-hanced pseudo-views, whereas geometry-consistent densifica-tion modifies the Gaussian representation in sparse regions according to local geometric consistency. The confidence weights are not used to control the densification rule. D. 2D-assisted Perceptual Enhancement 

In sparse-view reconstruction, raw reprojection from a few images provides weak and uneven constraints, which easily leads to blurry textures and inconsistent shading. Our 2D-assisted perceptual enhancement addresses this by rendering extra views from the coarse-scale Gaussian field, enhancing them with a diffusion-based 2D prior, and then filtering them with a confidence network that predicts pixel-level and view-level weights. These weights are used only to reweight the color and perceptual losses, so the fine-scale Gaussian field is trained under enhanced yet selective supervision that strengthens geometrically consistent, cross-view stable content while suppressing unreliable regions. 

Self-generated perceptual supervision. Due to incomplete perspective coverage, 3D Gaussians learned from sparse views often produce blurry or inconsistent regions. A pre-trained 2D prior, however, can restore missing details and yield more realistic images, even for coarse multi-view renderings. Motivated by this, we follow the per-scene enhancement strategy and inject 3D noise ϵs into Gaussian attributes to render degraded/reference pairs from the current scene. We then adapt only the LoRA [67] parameters of a ControlNet [68] enhancement module based on Stable Diffusion v1.5 [69]; the diffusion backbone remains frozen throughout this adaptation and during reconstruction. E. Geometry-Consistent Densification 

Beyond providing extra view information through percep-tual enhancement, we further refine Gaussian optimization in the refined field to improve geometric continuity. In 3DGS-based reconstruction [16], traditional Adaptive Density Control (ADC) adjusts density from local sparsity and inter-view con-sistency but often fails in sparse regions with weak gradients, where naive interpolation from few neighbors produces gaps or distortions, especially in complex or very low-density areas. To address this, we introduce a normal-weighted interpolation strategy that uses neighboring normals to place new Gaussians along the underlying surface, improving detail completion while preserving local structure. Specifically, we voxelize the 3D space into fixed-size cells and count the number of Gaussians in each voxel. Voxels with fewer than a threshold τ points are marked as sparse, so optimization can concentrate on these regions and avoid unnecessary computation. Let Drob denote the robust scene extent, computed as the 95th percentile of pairwise distances among Gaussian centers after removing extreme outliers. We set the voxel size to

neighborhood N (pi, r ) and estimate its normal ni by applying PCA to the neighboring points. For each neighboring point 

pj ∈ N (pi, r ), a weighting function is defined based on the similarity in normal direction and spatial distance. The weight based on normal similarity is given by: 

wnormal = exp( −α∥ni − nj ∥2), (21) where nj is the normal of neighboring point pj , and α is a hyperparameter controlling the influence of normal similarity. The spatial distance weight is defined as: 

wdistance = exp( −β∥pi − pj ∥2), (22) where β is a hyperparameter controlling the impact of dis-tance. The final weight for interpolation between pi and pj is then defined as the product of these two weights: 

wij = wnormal · wdistance . (23) Using these weights, we perform a weighted average of the positions and attributes of neighboring points to compute the interpolated point’s position and attributes. This interpolation operation is applied to every point in the sparse region, resulting in an interpolated region with twice the original density. This normal-based weighted interpolation strategy effectively preserves the geometric consistency within sparse voxels, generating new points that conform to the original geometric structure and mitigate distortions that may arise from arbitrary interpolation. IV. E XPERIMENTS 

A. Implementation Detail 

Our reconstruction framework extends 3D Gaussian Splat-ting (3DGS) [16] and casts sparse-view reconstruction as two-stage hierarchical geometry-perception feedback. The coarse field is optimized for 4,000 iterations, with standard adaptive density control every 100 iterations from iteration 500 to 

3,500 and opacity reset at iterations 1,500 and 3,000 . This stage uses ZoeDepth [70] as translation- and scale-invariant monocular depth supervision. After coarse reconstruction, de-graded/reference image pairs are constructed from the input views and only the LoRA parameters of the ControlNet / Stable Diffusion v1.5 enhancement module are adapted for 

500 optimization steps, while the diffusion backbone remains frozen. The fine Gaussian field then inherits only spatial means and RGB initialization from the coarse field and is optimized for 6,000 iterations. Enhanced pseudo-views are refreshed every K = 500 fine-stage iterations, and geometry-consistent densification is applied during the fine stage. The total Gaussian optimization budget is 10 ,000 iterations per scene, and the measured per-scene reconstruction time is approximately 10.5 minutes (632 s) at 779 × 520 on a single NVIDIA A100 GPU. V. C ONCLUSION 

In this paper, we present HierGF for sparse-view 3D re-construction, trained with a two-stage geometry-perception paradigm that renders pseudo-views from a coarse Gaussian field, refines them with a frozen-backbone, LoRA-adapted diffusion prior and confidence weighting, and uses normal-weighted densification to preserve surface continuity in under-sampled regions. On three primary sparse-view benchmarks, with additional quantitative comparisons on LLFF and DTU, HierGF improves reconstruction quality and cross-view con-sistency from few inputs. The revised efficiency analysis further shows that HierGF completes per-scene reconstruction in 632 s and post-optimization rendering in 70 s on the Mip-NeRF360 kitchen 4-view setting using a single NVIDIA A100 GPU.
