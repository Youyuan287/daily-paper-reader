## ABSTRACT 

Primitive-based 3D segmentation offers a compact and explicit alternative to dense surface prediction, naturally supporting structural abstraction and boundary lo-calization. However, geometric decomposition alone does not determine how primitives should be organized into semantic parts: a single part may span mul-tiple primitives, while geometrically similar or touching primitives may belong to different parts. We therefore introduce RePart (Re inforcement Part Reason-ing), which formulates primitive-to-part organization as a finite-horizon Markov decision process and learns semantic organization through trajectory-level re-inforcement reasoning. RePart constructs a Composable Primitive Workspace 

from fine-grained superquadrics and applies a merge-and-stop policy whose deci-sions are optimized by their downstream effects on the resulting partition rather than local primitive compatibility. The inferred part identities are then mapped back to the original mesh through Boundary-Aware Surface Labeling , preserv-ing accurate surface boundaries beyond the primitive approximation. On Part-Net, RePart achieves the strongest results across all four aggregate partition metrics; on 3DCoMPaT++, it obtains the highest RI and SC without target-dataset fine-tuning. These results demonstrate that reinforcement reasoning pro-vides an effective mechanism for organizing geometric primitives into seman-tic parts while retaining dense segmentation accuracy. Code is available at https://github.com/EngineeringAI-LAB/RePart. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

3D part segmentation has advanced rapidly through complementary paradigms, including geometric primitive decomposition (Paschalidou et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022; Monnier et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023a;c), learned 3D representations (Ma et al., 2025b; Liu et al., 2025a; Hadgi et al., 2026), and semantic transfer from vision–language and multi-view foundation models (Liu et al., 2023b; Umam et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2026). These advances have improved the flexibility and generalization of 3D part understanding. Meanwhile, LLM-driven 3D agents and interactive systems (Qi et al., 2024) are broadening the role of segmentation: predicted parts increasingly serve not only as dense outputs, but also as entities for reasoning, selection, editing (Ganeshan et al., 2024), and manipulation (Geng et al., 2023). This shift demands representations that expose compact object structure for high-level reasoning while remaining grounded in the original geometry. Geometric primitives are well suited to these requirements, replacing dense surface elements with spatially coherent units that encode shape, scale, pose, orientation, and volumetric extent. Primitive-based 3D decomposition has evolved from learned and deformable shape abstractions (Paschalidou et al., 2021; Monnier et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023a), to explicit primitive fitting (Wu et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023c), and structure-aware abstraction (Wang et al., 2025; Fedele et al., 2025). These methods naturally support Structural Abstraction : dense geometry is represented by compact explicit components at a tractable granularity (Paschalidou et al., 2019). Yet, as parts increasingly serve as meaningful entities rather than dense outputs, a third requirement becomes critical: Semantic Organization . Primitive decomposition is mainly governed by geometric fit and structural regularity, which do not determine how primitives compose into semantic parts. A single part may span multiple primitives, while nearby primitives with similar geometry may belong to different parts. Thus, the key unresolved problem is how to organize primitives into semantic parts (Luo et al., 2020). 

Semantic Organization cannot be reduced to independent or locally optimal grouping decisions. Pairwise classification assumes that mergeability can be judged in isolation, while greedy agglom-eration optimizes only immediate gains and cannot recover from early irreversible mistakes. Super-vised sequence learning is also ill-posed, because ground-truth segmentation specifies the desired fi-nal partition but not a unique merge trajectory. Starting from an over-segmented primitive partition, each merge changes group geometry, neighborhood relations, and the candidate actions available thereafter, so the value of an early decision is ultimately determined by the partition it enables. We therefore formulate semantic organization as a finite-horizon Markov decision process (Puterman, 1990). This formulation makes reinforcement learning (RL) well matched to the problem: it as-signs credit from the quality of the resulting partition back through the sequence of merge-and-stop decisions (Sutton et al., 1998), rather than requiring local pair labels or a prescribed reasoning path. Building on this formulation, we propose RePart (Re inforcement Part Reasoning), which places 

Semantic Organization at the center of primitive-based 3D segmentation and adapts both the reason-ing representation and dense output around it. Composable Primitive Workspace realizes Structural Abstraction by constructing a fine-grained SQ workspace with Marching-Primitives, then fixing the primitive geometry and surface correspondence to provide stable, composable units for reasoning without committing to the final part partition. Reinforcement-Learning Part Reasoning realizes Se-mantic Organization by casting SQ grouping as a merge-and-stop decision process and optimizing the policy with PPO, allowing each merge to be evaluated by its downstream effect on the evolving and final partition rather than local compatibility alone. Boundary-Aware Surface Labeling realizes 

Boundary Localization by transferring the inferred part identities to curvature-consistent mesh re-gions using grouped-SQ surface proximity and volumetric evidence, so that the final segmentation follows the original surface geometry instead of the approximate primitive boundaries. The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows: • We formulate primitive-to-part organization as a finite-horizon Markov decision process, enabling semantic grouping without requiring predefined merge sequences. • We introduce Reinforcement-Learning Part Reasoning to optimize merge-and-stop decisions through trajectory-level credit assignment rather than local primitive compatibility. • We couple this reasoning with a Composable Primitive Workspace and Boundary-Aware Sur-face Labeling , bridging compact primitive reasoning and precise mesh segmentation. 22 RELATED WORK 

3D Part Segmentation. Recent 3D part segmentation increasingly targets low-shot, class-agnostic, and open-world settings. Vision–language and multi-view foundation models transfer semantic cues to 3D (Liu et al., 2023b; Umam et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024; Tang et al., 2024), while large-scale 3D supervision and learned representations improve generalization and controllable granularity (Ma et al., 2025b; Liu et al., 2025a; Zhu et al., 2026; Hadgi et al., 2026; Su et al., 2026; Deng et al., 2026). These methods primarily predict dense part assignments. RePart instead reasons over com-pact geometric primitives, separating semantic organization from surface labeling. 

Primitive-Based 3D Abstraction. Primitive abstraction represents 3D geometry with compact, ex-plicit components, ranging from classical superquadric recovery (Leonardis et al., 1997) to learned, probabilistic, deformable, and differentiable formulations (Paschalidou et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2023a; Monnier et al., 2023). Later work models hierarchical structure or improves geometry-aware decomposition (Niu et al., 2022; Ruan et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025b), while MPS, Light-SQ, and SUPERDEC further develop SQ-based local abstraction and structural organization (Liu et al., 2023c; Wang et al., 2025; Fedele et al., 2025). GaussianGPT uses explicit Gaussian primitives as discrete tokens for scene generation (von Lützow et al., 2026). RePart instead treats primitives as composable reasoning units and focuses on organizing them into semantic parts. 

Sequential Primitive Organization. Classical mesh segmentation and hierarchical decomposition progressively merge geometric regions or components (Attene et al., 2006; Niu et al., 2022; Du et al., 2024; Ye et al., 2025). Learning to Group formulates bottom-up grouping as a contextual bandit with local mergeability rewards (Luo et al., 2020; Brehmer et al., 2020), while reinforcement learning has also been explored for hierarchical clustering and 3D reconstruction (Brehmer et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023). RePart differs by formulating primitive-to-part organization as a finite-horizon merge-and-stop Markov decision process, where irreversible decisions are optimized through their downstream effect on the resulting partition.

Merge-and-Stop Markov Decision Process. The composable primitive workspace provides com-pact geometric units, but it does not determine their part-level organization. Because a semantic part may span multiple SQs, the model must determine which current groups should share a part identity and when further consolidation should stop. These decisions are coupled: each irreversible merge changes group geometry and the candidates available later, while supervision specifies the desired terminal partition rather than a unique merge sequence. ## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets and splits. We train RePart on PartNet (Mo et al., 2019), grouping relevant categories into furniture, containers, and daily-use objects and sampling 300 shapes per group. We test on 200 total corresponding-category shapes from PartNet and 200 total from 3DCoMPaT++ (Slim et al., 2025). The latter is used only for testing, without target-dataset fine-tuning. 

Training. We first optimize one category-specialized policy per group over 200 epochs. The result-ing parameters initialize a joint fine-tuning stage on the union of the three training subsets for 100 epochs. All experiments are run on a single NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU. Unless otherwise specified, RePart denotes this jointly fine-tuned model. Appendix Figure A.1 reports the training rewards; to prevent reward-scale differences across category groups from joint training, we normalize advan-tages separately within each category, average its loss, and then macro-average the category losses. 

Baselines and metrics. We compare with Marching-Primitives (MPS) (Liu et al., 2023c), Part-Field (Liu et al., 2025a), P3-SAM (Ma et al., 2025a), SAMPart3D (Yang et al., 2024), and Primi-tiveAnything (PriMAny) (Ye et al., 2025). We report mean intersection-over-union (mIoU) (Yang et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025a) to measure overlap between matched predicted and ground-truth parts; Rand Index (RI) (Rand, 1971) to measure whether pairs of faces are consistently assigned to the same or different parts; Variation of Information (VoI) (Meil˘ a, 2007) to quantify the infor-mation difference between two partitions, including splitting and merging errors; and Segmentation Covering (SC) (Arbelaez et al., 2010) to measure how well predicted segments cover ground-truth regions. Following the PartField protocol, we report mIoU across its candidate granularities, with ground-truth labels used only for evaluation-time granularity selection. 4.2 MAIN RESULTS 

Quantitative Results. Tables 1 and 2 show consistently strong in-domain performance for RePart on PartNet across both category-level mIoU and partition-level metrics. RePart achieves the highest mIoU across all three category groups, reaching 0.4247 on furniture, 0.6456 on containers, and 0.5432 on daily-use objects. It also obtains the strongest aggregate results across all four partition metrics. Compared with the strongest competing results, RePart improves mIoU from 0.5249 to 0.5460 and SC from 0.6502 to 0.6891, while reducing VoI from 1.0213 to 0.8471. These results establish that the complete pipeline produces accurate and structurally coherent partitions across complementary evaluation criteria. On 3DCoMPaT++, RePart demonstrates cross-dataset transfer without fine-tuning, achieving the highest container mIoU (0.5149), while P3-SAM performs better on furniture and daily-use objects. Across all test shapes, RePart ranks first in RI (0.7575) and SC (0.5790), and second in both mIoU and VoI. Notably, RePart does not lead in transferred mIoU, but retains the strongest RI and SC. The transferred model therefore retains strong pairwise and region-level partition consistency despite the dataset shift. Category specialization and joint fine-tuning. We first compare the three category-specialized policies with the jointly fine-tuned policy. Table 3 reports category-level mIoU. Joint fine-tuning improves the average from 0.5375 to 0.5460 on PartNet and from 0.4255 to 0.4476 on 3DCoM-PaT++. Its gains on PartNet daily-use objects and 3DCoMPaT++ furniture and containers indicate that sharing structural experience across category groups improves transfer. 

Reinforcement-Learning Part Reasoning. We compare RePart-S, trained on 300 PartNet furniture shapes, with agglomerative policies sharing the same actor architecture, geometry-only SQ state, merge-and-stop action space, action mask, training data, and environment. Following Learning to Group (Luo et al., 2020), we adapt a contextual bandit and pairwise merge classifier. The former optimizes immediate transition rewards, while the latter learns from counterfactual one-step rewards over legal actions. Myopic-PPO sets γ = 0 and λGAE = 0 , removing temporal bootstrapping and future rewards, while Greedy-BC uses locally optimal merge trajectories as supervision. These controls isolate the effect of optimizing multi-step returns. Table 4 shows that the contextual bandit and Myopic-PPO struggle to learn grouping under this envi-ronment, indicating that immediate rewards alone provide insufficient guidance for the irreversible merge sequence. These results support optimizing multi-step returns for SQ grouping rather than treating merges as independent or locally optimal decisions. 

Boundary-Aware Surface Labeling. We isolate Boundary-Aware Surface Labeling by fixing the terminal SQ groups and varying only their transfer to mesh faces. These results support separating Semantic Organization from Boundary Localization .

## 5 CONCLUSION AND DISCUSSION 

We introduced RePart , a framework for Semantic Organization in 3D part segmentation that treats geometric primitives as reasoning units rather than final parts. RePart formulates primitive grouping as a finite-horizon merge-and-stop process, using trajectory-level reinforcement learning to optimize decisions by their downstream effects on the final partition, while Boundary-Aware Surface Label-ing maps inferred part identities back to the mesh. Experiments on PartNet and 3DCoMPaT++ 9support our core claim that multi-step reinforcement reasoning effectively organizes a fixed primi-tive workspace into coherent part-level structure, rather than primitive decomposition alone yielding semantic parts. Comparisons with local and myopic objectives further show the benefit of opti-mizing sequential decisions. Compact geometric primitives thus provide an intermediate reasoning space between dense geometry and semantic structure. RePart separates structural abstraction, part organization, and boundary localization. The fixed MPS-derived workspace provides a stable basis for sequential reasoning, but also ties performance to the quality of the initial decomposition. Our current study focuses on 900 shapes across three category groups; extending the action space and training coverage may further improve flexibility and generalization.

Candidate neighborhood size. The parameter merge_neighbor_k controls how many geo-metrically nearest groups are considered for each current group. B.3 ASYMMETRIC TRAINING SIGNALS 

The standard asymmetric model uses label-derived information only to improve training. The per-primitive privileged statistics describe label purity, ambiguity, and fragmentation, while the privi-leged global features summarize target-count mismatch and terminal grouping quality. The critic combines these signals with a detached actor representation. Detachment prevents critic-side su-pervision from directly changing the actor encoder, while the improved value estimate can still re-duce noise in PPO advantages. B.4 FUTURE WORK : LLM- AND AGENT -B ASED POLICY GENERATION 

The Composable Primitive Workspace separates semantic organization from dense surface predic-tion and is not tied to a particular policy architecture. This suggests exploring pretrained large language models (LLMs) or multimodal agents as alternatives to the current PPO-based policy in Reinforcement-Learning Part Reasoning. Compact primitives and explicit group relations could provide a more manageable basis for such models than direct reasoning over dense mesh elements. Related work on language-guided shape editing illustrates the potential of combining foundation models with geometric representations (Ganeshan et al., 2024). In our setting, an LLM or agent might guide merge-and-stop decisions while retaining the Composable Primitive Workspace and Boundary-Aware Surface Labeling, potentially avoiding task-specific RL training. Such an alternative may draw on broader knowledge of object composition and reduce dependence on the limited category coverage of our training set, offering a possible route toward better general-ization to unfamiliar structures. However, broader prior knowledge does not guarantee geometrically consistent grouping. Reliable stopping and label-free feedback remain open questions, since our cur-rent reward and privileged critic depend on ground-truth annotations unavailable at deployment. We have not implemented or evaluated this extension; its effectiveness and computational cost relative to PPO remain topics for future work. 

## C BOUNDARY -A WARE SURFACE LABELING 

We ablate the two components of Boundary-Aware Surface Labeling that transfer terminal SQ groups back to mesh faces. All variants reuse exactly the same grouped-SQ predictions, raw meshes, 19 SQ surface samples, and ground-truth face correspondences, so the comparison isolates Boundary-Aware Surface Labeling rather than Reinforcement-Learning Part Reasoning. We evaluate three increasingly structured variants on the daily-use, furniture, and container groups: • Facewise nearest SQ assigns each face centroid independently to the group with the nearest sampled SQ surface point. • + Curvature atoms first over-segments the mesh along high-dihedral adjacencies. Valid prox-imity votes are aggregated within each atom, while rejected atoms are resolved by the common nearest-surface fallback. SQ volumetric rescue is disabled in this variant. • + SQ volumetric evidence is the complete Boundary-Aware Surface Labeling module. It re-considers rejected atoms using the analytic signed-distance evidence of the grouped SQs before applying the same fallback to any remaining rejected atom. For reproducibility, each SQ surface is sampled on a 72 × 36 parameter grid. The atom bound-ary threshold is the 90th percentile of adjacent-face dihedral angles, clipped to [6 ◦, 12 ◦], followed by one label-smoothing iteration. We use uniform vote counts, a normalized face-distance thresh-old of 0.04 , a maximum distant-face fraction of 0.6, and a minimum atom size of one face. For volumetric rescue, an atom is accepted when at least half of its centroids support one group with an-alytic SQ signed distance no greater than 0.005 . This evidence is evaluated only for rejected atoms and therefore does not overwrite reliable proximity assignments. If both tests fail, an unrestricted nearest-surface majority vote assigns the whole atom. All partition metrics are computed after this common fallback over the same complete set of mesh faces with valid ground-truth labels; no unas-signed prediction is excluded. Final coverage is therefore 100% for every variant. We additionally report the fraction of faces assigned by fallback as a diagnostic of how often the preceding stages remain unresolved.
