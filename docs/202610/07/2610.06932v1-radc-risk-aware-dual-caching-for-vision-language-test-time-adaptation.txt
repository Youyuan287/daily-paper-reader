ABSTRACT 

Cache-based test-time adaptation (TTA) for vision-language mod-els is often hindered by background bias in global representations and unreliable entropy-based cache admission under representation variations. To address these limitations, we propose RADC, which enhances prototype learning through reliable dual caching. RADC introduces a Semantic Foreground Cache that aggregates category-consistent spatial evidence from CLIP representations, yielding fore-ground prototypes that complement the global cache while mitigat-ing background interference. To reliably manage both caches, Gaus-sian Risk Admission models multi-view representations as diagonal Gaussian distributions and jointly considers class separation and fea-ture uncertainty to prioritize reliable cache candidates. RADC inte-grates zero-shot logits with complementary global- and foreground-cache predictions for robust inference. Extensive experiments on cross-domain and out-of-distribution benchmarks demonstrate con-sistent state-of-the-art performance. 

Index Terms — Vision-language models, Test-time adaptation 

1. INTRODUCTION 

Vision-language models (VLMs), such as CLIP [1], have demon-strated strong zero-shot recognition capabilities through large-scale image-text pretraining, and can be further adapted to downstream tasks through post-training [2–5]. Nevertheless, their performance often degrades when the test distribution deviates from the pretrain-ing data. Test-time adaptation (TTA) [6] addresses this issue by exploiting unlabeled test samples during inference. Existing ap-proaches adapt prompts or prototypes [7, 8], while recent cache-based methods retrieve historical test representations to refine pre-dictions [9, 10]. Since cached samples are repeatedly involved in subsequent retrieval and prototype construction, the quality and reli-ability of cached representations are critical to effective online adap-tation. Despite their effectiveness, existing cache-based TTA methods face two key limitations. First, they typically construct caches from global image representations, which inevitably entangle category-relevant foreground information with sample-specific backgrounds. Consequently, irrelevant contextual patterns may accumulate in cached prototypes and repeatedly bias subsequent predictions. Sec-ond, cache admission is commonly determined by prediction con-fidence, typically favoring low-entropy samples [8–11]. However, sample-wise entropy may favor overconfident predictions, motivat-ing reliable caching that exploits complementary visual evidence

while accounting for representation uncertainty under view and localization variations. To this end, we propose RADC, a risk-aware dual-cache adap-tation framework for vision-language models. RADC complements the global cache with a Semantic Foreground Cache (SFC), forming a dual-cache architecture that preserves both holistic and category-relevant visual evidence. Given the candidate classes predicted for each test view, SFC first filters out hypotheses that are semantically inconsistent with the Top-1 prediction. It then independently local-izes each retained class using CLIP spatial contributions and aggre-gates the resulting class-wise masks to extract foreground represen-tations. In this way, the foreground cache provides category-relevant prototypes complementary to the global cache while reducing per-sistent background interference. Reliable dual caching further requires identifying which rep-resentations are sufficiently stable to contribute to prototype con-struction. We therefore introduce Gaussian Risk Admission (GRA), which models multi-view representations as diagonal Gaussian dis-tributions and evaluates each cache candidate by jointly considering class separation and feature uncertainty. For foreground representa-tions, GRA further incorporates localization uncertainty induced by spatial selection. The resulting risk scores govern admission and re-tention in both the global and foreground caches, prioritizing reliable representations for subsequent retrieval and prototype construction. By combining complementary dual-cache evidence with risk-aware admission, RADC enables robust test-time inference under distribu-tion shifts. In summary, our main contributions are as follows: (1) We pro-pose RADC, a risk-aware dual-cache framework that complements global representations with category-relevant foreground evidence for robust vision-language test-time adaptation; (2) We introduce SFC to construct foreground prototypes from semantically consis-tent spatial evidence, together with GRA to reliably manage both caches by jointly considering class separation and representation un-certainty; (3) Extensive experiments on cross-dataset and out-of-distribution benchmarks demonstrate consistent state-of-the-art per-formance with both RN50 and ViT-B/16 backbones.

2.3. Gaussian Risk Admission 

Prediction entropy reflects output confidence but does not capture the stability of the underlying representation. We therefore propose Gaussian Risk Admission (GRA) to evaluate each cache candidate using both its class margin and feature uncertainty. We model the visual evidence from selected views as a diagonal Gaussian distribution z ∼ N (μ, diag( σ2)) . Given a fixed pseudo-label y, we compare it with each class c in the text embedding space. Let tc and ty denote their corresponding text prototypes, and define the discriminative direction between them as dc = tc − ty . Implementation Details. We implement our method with frozen CLIP-RN50 and CLIP-ViT-B/16 backbones. For each image, we re-tain the 10% lowest-entropy views among the original and 63 Aug-Mix views. SFC extracts spatial contributions from the last three ViT attention layers or the RN50 AttentionPool2d layer. We set (τs, κ, δ, ϵ, λ b) = (0 .7, 10 , 0.5, 10 −6, 0.1) , with a foreground cache capacity of 16, αf = 2 , and βf = βg . All settings are fixed across datasets, and experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.

4. CONCLUSION 

We propose RADC, a risk-aware dual-cache framework for robust vision-language test-time adaptation. By complementing global rep-resentations with category-relevant foreground evidence and evalu-ating cache candidates through Gaussian risk, RADC retains more reliable and complementary representations for prototype learn-ing. This enables robust adaptation while mitigating background interference and representation uncertainty. Extensive experiments on cross-dataset and out-of-distribution benchmarks with RN50 and ViT-B/16 confirm its effectiveness, demonstrating consistent improvements over state-of-the-art methods.
